The short answer

Munich for this window.
Vienna when you can give it four days.

Both cities are wonderful in early September. The difference is arithmetic: Munich is six hours from Interlaken West with one change; Vienna is eleven. On a Tuesday-to-Thursday trip that is the difference between two full days on the ground and one.

1

Time. Munich: arrive 11:16 Tuesday, leave 12:32 or 14:32 Thursday. Vienna: arrive 17:32 at the earliest, leave 10:28 Thursday.

2

Money. Munich’s fare is verified at CHF 107 one-way. Vienna will run roughly CHF 130–185 each way. Hotels cost about the same in both at every tier.

3

Weather. Wednesday is wet everywhere (Munich gets thunderstorms, Vienna showers). Either city needs one museum day, and both have great ones.

If the Staatsoper is the point, Vienna still wins: Adriana Lecouvreur on Tuesday night is reachable on the 06:34 train. See page 4.

Option A

Vienna

The grandest city in German-speaking Europe, built for an empire of fifty million and now inhabited by two. Nowhere else does a Tuesday night at the opera feel this normal.

1

Density of masterpieces. Klimt at the Belvedere, Bruegel and Vermeer at the Kunsthistorisches, Schönbrunn’s 1,441 rooms, all inside a 20-minute tram ride.

2

Coffee-house life. A melange and a newspaper at Café Central is a legitimate two-hour plan. Sachertorte is not a cliché, it is lunch.

3

The Staatsoper. A different opera every night of your stay, seats from €88, and it is 500 metres from the Sacher.

Vienna · the shortlist

Six things worth the eleven hours

Schönbrunn

≈ €30 grand tour

Habsburg summer palace. Go at 08:30 opening, then climb to the Gloriette for the view. Gardens are free.

Belvedere

≈ €18

Klimt’s The Kiss in the Upper Belvedere, Schiele and Kokoschka next door. The rainy-Wednesday move.

Stephansdom

Nave free · tower ≈ €7

Gothic heart of the city. 343 steps up the south tower for the tiled-roof close-up; the catacombs are the quieter tour.

Naschmarkt

Free

1.5 km of stalls, Levantine snacks and Viennese vegetable stands. Lunch at Neni or a Käsekrainer at a stand.

Riesenrad, Prater

≈ €14

The 1897 Ferris wheel from The Third Man. Twenty minutes in a wooden cabin at golden hour; the Prater woods for a run.

Coffee-house hour

Torte + melange ≈ €14

Café Sacher for the original torte, Café Central for the room, Café Prückel or Sperl for the locals’ version.

If there is time: Hofburg and the Sisi Museum · Kunsthistorisches Museum (Bruegel room) · MuseumsQuartier courtyards · Karlskirche · a Heuriger evening in Grinzing · Hundertwasserhaus.

Option B

Munich

Bavaria’s easy-going capital: a Gothic square, a river you can surf, and the biggest urban park in Europe with a beer garden at its centre. Six hours from Interlaken, door to door.

1

Two full days instead of one. Arrive for lunch on Tuesday, leave after lunch on Thursday. Nothing about the trip is rushed.

2

Outdoors first. The Eisbach surfers, the Englischer Garten and the Viktualienmarkt are all free, and Tuesday will be 30° and dry.

3

World-class indoors for Wednesday. Deutsches Museum, the Pinakotheken and the Residenz absorb a thunderstorm without effort.

Munich · the shortlist

Six things, most of them free

Marienplatz

Free · tower ≈ €6

Neo-Gothic Rathaus and the Glockenspiel at 11:00, 12:00 and 17:00. Climb St Peter’s (Alter Peter) for the view on page 9.

Eisbach surfers

Free

A standing wave on a city stream, surfed year-round at the edge of the Englischer Garten. Ten minutes of watching is compulsory.

Englischer Garten

Free · Maß ≈ €11

Bigger than Central Park. Monopteros for the skyline, the Chinesischer Turm beer garden for a Maß and Obatzda under the chestnuts.

Viktualienmarkt

Free

Two-hundred-year-old food market off Marienplatz: cheese stalls, Weißwurst before noon, a beer garden under the maypole.

Nymphenburg

≈ €12 palace · park free

Baroque summer palace with a canal, swans and the Gallery of Beauties. Tram 17 from the Hauptbahnhof, 15 minutes.

Frauenkirche

Free · tower ≈ €10

The twin onion domes on every skyline. The south tower reopened with a lift; on a clear day you see the Alps.

For the stormy Wednesday: Deutsches Museum (≈ €15, the largest science museum anywhere) · Alte Pinakothek and Pinakothek der Moderne (≈ €9 each) · Residenz and Cuvilliés Theatre (≈ €10) · BMW Welt (free) · Hofbräuhaus, if only once.

Before you book

Five clicks, in order

  • Decide by tonight: hotel rates were live on 5 Sept and the Ruby, Platzl and 25hours rooms are the ones that vanish first.
  • Train on sbb.ch: Interlaken West → München Hbf, 08.09.2026, and the return 10.09.2026 (EC 192, 12:32). Confirm CHF 107 each way; a saver fare is train-specific, so pick the departure you will actually make.
  • If you go for Vienna instead: same search to Wien Hbf, 06:34 out and 10:28 back, then buy Adriana Lecouvreur on wiener-staatsoper.at the same minute.
  • Book the hotel on the official site once you have the Google price as a reference; Sacher, Imperial, Bayerischer Hof and Ruby usually match or beat the OTA rate with breakfast thrown in.
  • Pack for 30° on Tuesday and a storm on Wednesday. Museums, opera and beer halls do not care about the weather; the Englischer Garten does, so it goes on Tuesday.

Assumptions: one adult, 2nd class, no rail pass. With a Halbtax the Swiss legs halve (≈ CHF 35 saved each way to Zürich). Times are the published timetable and can change on the day; check the app the evening before.
